Hot on the heels of a presentation I give today in a ToxBank virtual meeting
on (Open) Standards (I'll blog the points as soon as possible), I just ran into
[this great blog post](http://weblog.clarkparsia.com/2010/05/26/another-reason-semantic-web-kicks-ass/)
by the makers of the OWL reasoner [Pellet](http://clarkparsia.com/pellet/) (license:GPL),
which we use in [Bioclipse](http://www.bioclipse.net). It is spot on on what
happens if you use Open Standards: "That's notable not only because it took
so little time, but also because of the long list of things that we weren’t
required to do in order to make it happen". This is not a new message. For example,
the [Quixote project](http://quixote.wikispot.org/) is doing the same thing
in computation chemistry, but important for people to understand: Open Standards
(quite like Open Source, and Open Data) are a game changer: they simply collaborating,
speeding up development, and changing the way we do things.
